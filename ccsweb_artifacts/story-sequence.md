# Native Boarding Schools & Adoption - IG/FB Story Sequence

**Theme:** "What they didn't teach us about Native boarding schools & adoption."

**Format:** 4-slide Instagram/Facebook Story
**Purpose:** Educational advocacy content about historical trauma and ongoing issues affecting Native communities
**Target:** General audience, particularly those unfamiliar with this history

---

## Slide 1 – Hook

**Visual Guidance:**
- Faded, sepia-toned or black-and-white photograph
- Historic image of Native children in boarding school uniforms at Carlisle Indian Industrial School (or similar institution)
- Somber, serious tone
- Could include subtle vignette effect to draw focus to the children's faces

**Text Overlay:**
```
Did you learn this in school?
```

**Design Notes:**
- Use large, bold, readable font (white or cream text with subtle shadow for contrast)
- Position text in lower third or upper third to avoid covering children's faces
- Keep visual simple and powerful - the image speaks volumes

---

## Slide 2 – Fact (Boarding Schools Era)

**Visual Guidance:**
- Close-up of a child's face from a historic boarding school photograph
- Black-and-white or sepia tone
- Focus on emotion - the child's eyes, expression
- NO graphic/violent imagery; maintain dignity and respect

**Text Overlay:**
```
Between 1819 and 1969, the U.S. ran 400+ Indian boarding schools in 37 states.

Native kids were taken from their families to "kill the Indian, save the man."

Source: U.S. Department of the Interior
```

**Design Notes:**
- Use clear, legible font
- Break text into readable chunks
- Include citation at bottom in smaller font
- Consider dark overlay on image (20-40% opacity) to ensure text readability
- Use neutral colors (white, cream, or light gray text)

**Additional Context:**
- The phrase "kill the Indian, save the man" is a direct quote from Richard Henry Pratt, founder of Carlisle Indian Industrial School
- This policy was cultural genocide - children had their hair cut, were forbidden from speaking their languages, and were punished for practicing their cultures

---

## Slide 3 – Adoption Era

**Visual Guidance:**
- Vintage newspaper clipping aesthetic
- Could be styled to look like a 1950s-1970s newspaper headline
- Muted colors (newsprint gray, sepia, cream backgrounds)
- Typography: typewriter or newspaper-style fonts

**Text Overlay:**
```
Later, through adoption and foster programs, as many as 1 in 3 Native children were removed from their homes—most placed with non-Native families.

Source: visionmakermedia.org
```

**Headline Suggestion (optional decorative element):**
```
"ADOPTION"
or
"THE ADOPTION ERA"
```

**Design Notes:**
- Newspaper aesthetic creates visual variety in the story sequence
- Could include torn edge effects or aged paper texture
- Maintain readability with high contrast
- Citation at bottom in smaller font

**Historical Context:**
- This refers to programs like the Indian Adoption Project (1958-1967) and broader child welfare removals
- The Indian Child Welfare Act (ICWA) was passed in 1978 to address these abuses
- Some estimates suggest 25-35% of all Native children were removed from their homes during peak years

---

## Slide 4 – Resilience & Call to Action

**Visual Guidance:**
- Contemporary, vibrant, colorful photograph
- Options:
  - Native community gathering or powwow (regalia, dancing, drums)
  - Community food sovereignty event (traditional foods, cooking, gathering)
  - Language teaching/learning scene (elders with youth)
  - Modern Native families together
- Warm, hopeful, vibrant tone - contrast with earlier slides
- Example style: like scenes from PBS documentaries "Gather," "Dawnland," or "Sovereign Innovations"

**Text Overlay:**
```
Native communities are still here—teaching languages, reclaiming foodways, fighting for their children.

Learn more: search "Generations Stolen," "Dawnland," and "Gather."

🔁 Share to honor survivors.
```

**Design Notes:**
- Celebratory but respectful tone
- Text should feel uplifting but not dismissive of trauma
- Use readable font with good contrast
- Consider green, earth-tone, or warm color palette for text
- Make CTA clear and actionable

**Recommended Documentaries:**
- **"Generations Stolen"** - About boarding school survivors
- **"Dawnland"** - Maine Wabanaki child welfare crisis and ICWA
- **"Gather"** - Native food sovereignty movement (on Netflix)

**Additional Resources:**
- PBS's Native America series
- National Native American Boarding School Healing Coalition
- IllumiNative organization

## Technical Specifications for Story Posts

### Instagram/Facebook Story Dimensions:
- **Resolution:** 1080 x 1920 pixels (9:16 aspect ratio)
- **Safe zones:** Keep important text/elements within center 1080 x 1680px area
- **File format:** JPG or PNG (under 30MB)
- **Duration:** Each slide should display for 5-7 seconds if auto-advancing

### Accessibility Considerations:
- Include alt text for each image
- Ensure text has sufficient contrast ratio (WCAG AA: at least 4.5:1)
- Use clear, readable fonts (minimum 24pt equivalent)
- Avoid rapid transitions that could trigger seizures

### Hashtag Suggestions (if posting to feed or Stories with text):


## Design Tips

1. **Visual Hierarchy:**
   - Slide 1: HOOK - Minimal text, powerful image
   - Slides 2-3: FACTS - More text, clear sources
   - Slide 4: ACTION - Balance of text and uplifting imagery

2. **Color Palette:**
   - Slides 1-3: Muted, historical (sepia, black/white, newsprint gray)
   - Slide 4: Vibrant, contemporary (earth tones, warm colors, rich hues)

3. **Emotional Arc:**
   - Slides 1-3: Recognition of trauma and injustice
   - Slide 4: Resilience, hope, and action

4. **Typography:**
   - Use 2-3 fonts maximum across all slides
   - Ensure high contrast and readability on mobile
   - Consider sans-serif for body text, serif or display font for headlines

---

## Legal & Ethical Considerations

1. **Image Rights:**
   - Historic boarding school photos: Many are public domain (check Library of Congress, National Archives)
   - Contemporary photos: Ensure you have permission or use stock photos from Native photographers
   - Consider using services like Native Stock (nativestock.ca) or Navajo Stock Images

2. **Cultural Sensitivity:**
   - Avoid appropriating sacred imagery
   - Don't use images of ceremonies without explicit permission
   - Center Native voices - consider partnering with Native creators
   - If possible, have Native community members review before posting

3. **Platform Guidelines:**
   - Instagram/Facebook allow educational content about historical injustice
   - This content does not violate community standards (it's advocacy/education)
   - Be prepared for comments - moderate respectfully
